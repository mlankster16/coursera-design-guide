MODULE

Designing a Coursera Module

The Course page helped you identify the Course objectives, the knowledge and skills learners may need, the resources you already have, and possible Module groupings. Now, choose one Module and develop it further.

This happens in two stages. First you’ll design the learning experience: define focused Module objectives, identify a task that demonstrates them, and plan the explanation, practice, feedback, and support learners will need. Then you’ll build the Coursera experience, choosing the assets, tools, and formats that support each part of that learning path.

Writing Module objectives

Course objectives describe what learners should be able to do by the end of the Course. Module objectives break related Course objectives into smaller, achievable steps that learners can develop and demonstrate within one Module.

You might ask

Which parts of the Course objectives will this Module help learners develop?

Rather than asking

What topic should this Module cover?

A Module might introduce a concept, guide learners through a process, make an expert's reasoning visible, or give learners an opportunity to apply a skill. Write 1–3 objectives that describe what learners will be able to demonstrate by the end of the Module.

Turning Module objectives into a learning task

In Module 2: Identifying and Evaluating Business Data, learners are working toward three Module objectives:

  • ·Identify the data needed to investigate a business question.
  • ·Evaluate an available dataset for relevance, completeness, and basic quality concerns.
  • ·Document limitations that could affect the proposed analysis.

To plan the final task, consider what learners would need to do to demonstrate each Module objective:

Module objectives and the evidence of learning for each
Module objective Evidence of learning
Identify the data needed Specify the variables, records, time period, and level of detail needed to investigate a business question
Evaluate an available dataset Compare the available data with those requirements and identify concerns
Document limitations Explain how missing, incomplete, or poor-quality data could affect the proposed analysis

These actions can be brought together in one meaningful task. Learners could receive a business question and an unfamiliar dataset, inspect the data in relation to the question, and document whether the dataset is fit for the proposed analysis.

For this Module, we will call that task a Data Fitness Review.

The final task

Complete a Data Fitness Review that evaluates whether an available dataset is suitable for addressing a business question and documents concerns that could affect the proposed analysis.

Now work backward from that task:

Backward-planning questions and the decision for this Module
Planning question Decision for this Module
What does a successful response look like? The review identifies the data the question requires, evaluates the dataset’s relevance and completeness, notes basic quality concerns, and explains how any limitations could affect the analysis.
What do learners already know? Learners can recognize common parts of a dataset, including records, fields, and values.
What do learners still need to learn? They need criteria for evaluating data in relation to a particular question and practice explaining why a limitation matters.
Where might they struggle? Learners may focus on whether the data appears clean without noticing that essential variables, time periods, or levels of detail are missing.
How will they know they are on track? A worked example, data-quality checklist, guiding questions, and explanatory feedback will help them compare their reasoning with the evaluation criteria.

These decisions establish what the Module must prepare learners to do. The next step is to arrange the necessary explanation, modeling, practice, and feedback into a coherent learning path.

Choosing and sequencing the stages

Modules do not need to follow one fixed structure. Some topics require more explanation, while others benefit from repeated practice, multiple examples, or opportunities to make and correct mistakes.

These stages below are a planning guide, not a required sequence. Depending on the Module, a stage may be brief, repeated, combined with another stage, or unnecessary. 

1 Frame Introduce what learners will do, why it matters, and how it connects to the Module objectives.
2 Explain Introduce the essential concepts, process, or framework learners will need.
3 Model Show how an expert approaches the task, applies the process, or makes decisions. Think aloud where helpful.
4 Practice Give learners an opportunity to try part or all of the task with appropriate guidance.
5 Give feedback Help learners recognize what is working, correct misunderstandings, and decide what to try next. Feedback may include automated responses, answer explanations, worked examples, checklists, or prompts for self-review.
6 Apply or assess Ask learners to use what they have learned in a meaningful context. This may be an auto-graded assessment or an ungraded activity with clear guidance for self-checking.

These stages may repeat or overlap. For example, learners may move through explanation, modeling, practice, and feedback several times before completing the final task.

Mapping and supporting the learning path

The backward-planning process established that learners will complete a Data Fitness Review. To succeed, they will need to identify the data a business question requires, evaluate an available dataset against those requirements, and explain how its limitations could affect an analysis.

The learning path prepares learners for each part of that task:

Stages of the learning path, the learner experience, and each stage’s role
Stage Learner experience Role in the learning path
Frame Encounter a scenario in which a team has customer data but is unsure whether it can answer a particular business question Establishes why the quality and usefulness of data must be judged in relation to its intended purpose
Explain Learn how relevance, completeness, level of detail, and basic quality affect whether data can support an analysis Introduces the criteria learners will use in the Data Fitness Review
Model Watch an expert identify what data a question requires, inspect a sample dataset, and explain their judgment aloud Makes the reasoning and documentation process visible
Practice Use the criteria and a checklist to inspect a second dataset and draft a supported conclusion Lets learners try the major parts of the final task with guidance
Give feedback Compare their observations with an annotated response and review explanations of concerns they may have missed Helps learners check their reasoning, correct misunderstandings, and recognize what a complete evaluation includes
Apply or assess Complete a Data Fitness Review for a new business question and dataset Asks learners to demonstrate the Module objectives independently
Each stage prepares learners for something the final task requires. The explanation introduces the evaluation criteria, the model shows how to apply and document them, the practice lets learners try the process with support, and the feedback helps them refine their judgment before working independently.

The path may still move among explanation, modeling, practice, and feedback more than once. What matters is that learners receive the preparation they need before being asked to complete the Data Fitness Review on their own.

Matching assets to learning needs

On the Course page, you identified the content and resources you already have, what may need to be developed, and questions about Coursera. Now use those notes to decide how each part of the Module’s learning path might be supported.

Begin with the learner’s experience rather than the format. A worked example might be delivered through a video or a reading. Practice might take place through an ungraded assessment, Coach Role Play, a programming assignment, an interactive plugin, or a Coursera Lab.

The same asset can serve different purposes. A video might explain a concept, demonstrate a process, or make expert reasoning visible. What matters is how the asset helps learners progress toward the Module objectives.

Choose a Learning Asset that fits the learning need

As you consider the options, ask:

  • Does the learner need to read, watch, listen, interact, converse, practice, or create?
  • Would an explanation, reusable reference, demonstration, guided practice, feedback, or opportunity to demonstrate the objective be most helpful?
  • Would this format add meaningful value, or might another asset serve the same purpose?
  • What instructions, preparation, or support will learners need to use the asset independently?
  • How will the asset connect to what learners encountered before and what they will do next?

You might ask

What does the learner need to do next, and which Coursera Learning Asset might support that need most effectively?

Rather than asking

These assets can be organized into a smaller number of Lessons. A Lesson is not a single asset; it is a purposeful segment of the learning path that may bring several assets together.

For example:

  • Lesson 1: Identify the data the question requires includes a Video, a Reading, and an ungraded assessment.
  • Lesson 2: Evaluate whether the data is fit for purpose includes two Videos, a Reading, an ungraded assessment, and a hands-on activity.
  • Lesson 3: Complete a Data Fitness Review includes a Video, an ungraded assessment, and the auto-graded quiz.

The Module meets Coursera’s asset expectations while keeping every selection connected to the learning path. The goal is not to minimize the number of assets, but to avoid adding assets that do not help learners progress toward the final task.

5 Step 5 of 5

Bring the Module plan together

Review the Module plan as a learner and as a blueprint for development.

Together with your Learning Experience Designer, review the Module plan and confirm that the learner experience, source materials, and development questions are clear enough to move forward. You do not need to resolve every question on your own; use this conversation to identify what is ready, what needs attention, and what you would like to explore together.

As you review the plan together, consider the following:

Learning experience

The Module has a clear focus and meaningful purpose for learners on Coursera.

Learners have a clear opportunity to demonstrate each Module objective.

Terms are used consistently across the Module objectives, learning path, Lessons, and Learning Assets.

Any prerequisite knowledge, tools, accounts, or sequence dependencies are clear.

Learners have more than one way to engage with important information when appropriate.

Any interaction or tool that may create an access barrier has been identified for discussion.

Accessibility and learner access

Text uses clear language, logical headings, and consistent formatting.

Links use meaningful text that makes sense outside the surrounding sentence.

Important images have alternative text.

Charts, diagrams, and data visualizations have a written explanation of their essential meaning.

Visual materials use accessible color contrast.

Video plans leave space for Coursera captions and describe important visual information aloud.

Documents shared with learners can be made digitally accessible.

Activities using tools, plugins, editors, consoles, or visualizations have an accessible alternative or a plan for addressing barriers.

Copyright and permissions

The source of each third-party text, image, video, dataset, software library, tool, or scenario is documented.

Creative Commons materials include the creator, title, source, license, and any changes made, unless the material is marked CC0.

Any reliance on fair use has been flagged for discussion with the CTL team.

Private, confidential, proprietary, or personally identifiable information has been removed or replaced with appropriate examples.

External tools, datasets, repositories, APIs, and software have been checked for access, licensing, privacy, and commercial-use conditions.

Best practice

Graded quiz

✕

Build the quiz around the Module objectives, rather than testing isolated facts from videos or readings. When the objective calls for application, interpretation, or decision-making, design questions that ask learners to apply, interpret, distinguish, or decide.

Provide useful feedback for both correct and incorrect responses. Feedback can explain why an answer works, clarify a misconception, or point learners toward what they may want to review.

Use the Graded Quiz Import Template in your Design Document when drafting quiz questions. Working in the template helps organize questions, answer options, correct answers, and feedback in a format that supports the Coursera build.
