What is a Coursera learning experience?

Coursera learning experiences are built in nested layers. A Specialization is made of Courses, each Course is made of Modules, and each Module is made of Learning Assets. You may develop a full Specialization or a single Course.

Who Coursera learners areAnd what makes this experience different+

Coursera learners come from around the world and bring different backgrounds, goals, and levels of experience. Many are working professionals studying on their own schedule, without an instructor present to answer questions in the moment.

Self-paced and applied

Courses are shorter than a semester and organized around skills learners can use.

A broad audience

Professionals, students, career changers, or people exploring a new subject.

Support built in

Explanation, practice, feedback, and assessment are part of the experience itself.

Public and global

Materials may reach learners beyond Duke and remain available beyond a single term.

Each Coursera layer, the planning question it answers, and the closest part of a Duke course
In your Duke course On Coursera Planning question
Your whole course: a semester of materialA SpecializationWhat larger goal are learners working toward?
A unit or theme: often several weeks of your syllabusA CourseWhat meaningful skills should learners develop?
A topic inside that unit: a concept, method, or skillA ModuleWhat smaller learning steps will help learners get there?
A lecture, reading, activity, or assessmentLearning AssetsWhat experience will best support each step?

Terms like Specialization, Course, Module, and Learning Assets are capitalized in this guide to distinguish Coursera learning products from a for-credit course or class session.
